# Richard's UI taste

Richard Borcsik's taste, collected from the owner decisions of nine UI climbs on his assistant app.
Pick it for a run by asking for "Richard's taste". It replaces the [default taste](taste.md),
and it is written in the form described in [custom-taste.md](custom-taste.md).

- **Compact marks over words.** State shows as a tint, a dot or the working dots, never as a
  spelled-out status ("Needs you", "Working", "Done"). No caption explains what is already
  obvious ("It's history").
- **Attention is earned.** One mark per state, one place per decision, one working indicator.
  System information (what happened, records, logs) never takes the accent colour.
- **Hide the machinery.** Internal states, tool names, elapsed time and the parts working
  behind the scenes stay out of view; the screen shows what is being done and what came of it.
- **Each surface has one job.** A log is a log, a decision lives in one place, and the same thing
  is never asked for twice on one screen.
- **Grounded.** No serif or second typeface, no gradients, coloured light, floating text, or
  content centred in empty space. Contrast stays high, and is measured when in doubt.
- **Native behaviour, own look.** Platform patterns and materials (system sheets, translucent
  bars, real navigation), never a solid wash over a material. Desktop follows desktop
  conventions, not a stretched phone layout.
- **Match the product's own components.** A new sheet, card or panel looks like the ones the
  product already has, and one treatment covers every card of a kind.
- **Nothing moves under the pointer.** Controls keep their place while the user acts on them, and
  hovers and overlays never shift the list.
- **Details are checked literally.** Connector lines join, text never clips when the row has
  room, and nothing is sliced at an edge.
- **Content is the design.** Sample data reads as finished outcomes with no contradictions;
  replies lead with the takeaway, and titles use the row's full width before they wrap.
- **One calm hierarchy.** Headings, cards and the type inside them read as one system, built from
  the existing type styles, never a new visual language per screen.
- **Respect the device.** Times follow its 12 or 24 hour setting, the app follows its light or
  dark theme, and the theme not being judged still holds up.
